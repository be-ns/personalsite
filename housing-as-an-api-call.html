Case study · San Mateo, California

Housing as an API call

One address in. Out: five homes by right where two stand today, and the most the lot can cost before building them stops paying. I ran the call with an AI research agent, from the zoning code to the capital stack.

By Ben Siverly · November 2026 · 9 min read

The house at 1xx Hobart Ave is a 1953 duplex in San Mateo. Two 2-bed units sit at the ends of one long roof, with two 2-car garages between them. The lot is 8,750 sq ft and zoned for apartments.

California now lets an owner add homes to a lot like this without a public hearing. Most owners never learn how many, because the answer takes a zoning read, a site plan, rent comps, a cost estimate, and a pro forma. On a project that adds three homes, nobody pays for that work.

So I treated it as an API call. I gave an AI research agent the address and worked the problem with it in one long conversation. Here is the request. The response follows, one field at a time.

response.entitlements

What the law allows

Each rule runs like a test. Five pass, one needs review, and two fail. The failures shape the answer as much as the passes.

rules.run(parcel)8 rules

  1. Passdensity.r31 home per 2,500 sq ft on a 50-ft lot. 8,750 ÷ 2,500 = 3.5, so 3 primary homes.San Mateo Code 27.22.040
  2. Passadu.conversionAt least 1 ADU (accessory dwelling unit, an extra home on a lot that already has one) inside non-living space. Both garages become one home.Gov. Code 66323
  3. Passadu.detached1 detached ADU per existing unit, so 2.SB 1211
  4. Passadu.height18 ft to the top of the walls, 24 ft to the peak. Each ADU gets 2 stories.San Mateo Code 27.19, 2026 update
  5. Passadu.parkingNo replacement parking for a converted garage.Gov. Code 66322
  6. Reviewprimary.thirdA third primary home fits under the density cap. It needs normal zoning review, including parking.San Mateo Code 27.22
  7. Failsb684.small_lotUp to 10 homes on a small multifamily lot, but not where tenants lived in the last 5 years.SB 684
  8. Failadu.detached.thirdLegal once there are 3 primary homes. It does not fit inside the 4-ft setbacks, the strip an ADU must leave clear along the side and rear lot lines.Site fit
2Today
5By right
6At most

By right means city staff approve it with no hearing. The sixth home is the only one that goes through normal review.

response.market

What it rents for

Every home is priced at the upper third of San Mateo rents, $4.50 per sq ft a month. The city average was $4.26 in July 2026.

Unit 2 is the weak point. Hold it to $5,000 a month and the project earns 6.1% a year on its cost instead of 6.4%.

Small apartment buildings in San Mateo trade at roughly 4% to 5.5% cap rates. A cap rate is a year of a building’s rent, after expenses, over its price, so at 5% a buyer pays 20 times what the building nets in a year. A similar duplex on the same block listed at $2.6M in June 2026.

response.capital

If I raised it

Same deal with outside money. A construction loan at 65% of cost and 9%. Investors get an 8% preferred return first. After that they take 70% of the profit, and the GP, who runs the deal, takes 30%. Sell at month 24.

  • $457kProfit at sale
  • 17.2%Deal IRR
  • 14.6%LP net IRR, 1.31×
  • $237kGP take

IRR is the return per year. The LPs’ figure is after the GP’s fee and promote, and 1.31× means they get $1.31 back for every $1 they put in. The GP take, $236,640, is a $122k developer fee, a $76k promote (the GP’s 30% of the profit after the preferred return), and $38k earned on its own $122k. About half of it is fee.

Minimum in for the GP

  • Co-invest: $122k, which is 10% of the equity.
  • Cash at risk before LPs fund: $100k to $150k for the deposit, offering documents, design, and survey.
  • Lender test: a guarantor with net worth of 0.75 to 1.0 times the loan, and cash near 10% of it.

Where it breaks

  • A 5.5% exit cap drops the deal IRR to 4.6%.
  • Median rents, $4.00 per sq ft, turn the profit into a loss.
  • Hold and refinance: the bank lends about $2.02M against a $2.10M construction payoff. The GP adds $79k, and LPs earn about 3% cash.

response.verdict

The answer

  1. Pass

    As a home: pass

    Live in one unit and rent four. Net housing cost is about $6,100 a month at $1.58M.

  2. Thin

    As a sale: thin

    17% deal IRR at $1.58M. Half a point on the exit cap erases most of it.

  3. Pass

    For outside capital, at $1.40M or less: pass

    LPs net about 22% at $1.40M, and a refinance still hands back cash. They clear 15% up to about $1.56M, but past about $1.49M a refinance returns nothing.

  4. Fail

    As a hold: fail

    At today’s rates the new loan falls $79k short of paying off the construction loan, so no money goes back to investors, and they earn about 3% a year in cash.

The law already allows five homes here. The purchase price decides whether they get built. Next, the same call runs on every R3 lot in San Mateo, ranked by the price each one needs.

How the call ran

Ten steps, in order. Each one names its source.

  1. parcel.lookupParcel 034-382-xxx, 8,750 sq ft, R3, built 1953.County records
  2. zoning.resolve1 home per 2,500 sq ft on a 50-ft lot, so 3 primary homes.San Mateo Code 27.22.040
  3. law.overlayOne garage ADU, two detached ADUs, 18-ft walls. SB 684 blocked by recent tenancy.San Mateo Code 27.19.070 · San Mateo County on SB 684
  4. site.fitFootprint traced from an aerial photo. A seventh home fails the 4-ft setbacks.Aerial photo, county square footage
  5. massing.renderThree scenarios, drawn as axonometric massing and a site plan.Generated on this page
  6. market.rents$4.50 per sq ft for the upper third. Cap rates of 4% to 5.5%.Zumper · Rentometer · RZ Advisors

Contents

Limits

  • The lot size and footprint come from an aerial photo, not a survey.
  • Rents, costs, and cap rates are estimates from public sources as of mid 2026.
  • San Mateo Planning would need to confirm ADU heights, tree limits, and parking for the third primary home.
  • The renders are rough massing, not a design.
  • The street number and the end of the parcel number are withheld. The owner and tenants did not ask to be in a case study.
