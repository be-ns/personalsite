### The Central Valley

In rural places like the Central Valley, a lot of people live a long drive from whatever a product assumes is close by: the bank, the club, the office that takes certified funds. I keep them in mind. They keep turning up in the work: renters driving 15–30 minutes for certified funds, and players good enough for college soccer who grow up too far from the big clubs for a recruiter to see them.

### The Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma

Proud future ancestor of the Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma. I try to work like one: leave the people after me something they can read, trace and keep running without me. Written specs, numbers with their sources, systems the next person can follow.

### I left a job over the data

At Oracle the company was pulling together location data, browsing behavior and real-world signals to predict what people would buy. I had growing concerns about where that data came from and how it was being used, and they eventually led me to leave. If the data you have isn't data you should use, I'll say so.

### A recovering data scientist

I still count everything. Then I poke at the count. One number with its source beats five without one.

### I build my own things

An apparel company, back when. Brava, nights and weekends with my brother. Six free tools on this site that someone's AI agent can run. I'd rather show you a working thing than a deck.

### The unglamorous middle

The customer calls, the half-clean data, the code that has to actually run. That's where I'm useful, long before the demo.

### A few at a time

I take on a few clients at a time, on purpose. Each one gets real attention, and I stay close enough to the work to do it myself.
