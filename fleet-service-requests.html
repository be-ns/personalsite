      The key and the matcher are different things

      An Idempotency-Key is transport-layer: one client retrying one HTTP request. Deterministic, caller-generated, returns the cached response.

      The matcher is domain-layer: different people reporting the same real-world issue through channels no interface owns. Probabilistic, server-side, reversible.

      Conflating them is the bug at the center of this product. The key stops a double-tap. The matcher stops three people.

      What would change my mind

      • If duplicate intakes measure under 10 percent in the first instrumented week, the record problem is smaller than the calls suggest and shop coordination moves up.
      • If dispatchers reject more than a third of merge suggestions, the matcher is below the usefulness line and it drops to suggest-only while the factors get rebuilt.
      • If the severity downgrade rate runs past 10 percent, the classifier is miscalibrated and flags will start being ignored, which is worse than no flags.

      What this signal does not tell us

      Ticket themes say where the pain surfaces, not where it starts. The data to request next: intake volume by channel, time from report to first human touch, and how often the same defect reappears within 30 days of a closed request.

      The boundary follows the cost of being wrong. A false merge hides a real defect behind another ticket; a false split produces a duplicate that is annoying, visible, and self-correcting. Downgrade and close are one click for a dispatcher and the last line of defense before a defect disappears. Automating them buys seconds and sells the audit trail.
